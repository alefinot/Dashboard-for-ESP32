# Plain-English pinout sheet (drawing aid)

Everything here is read out of the board itself (`hardware/dashboard-plusplus-carrier.kicad_pcb`,
generated by `hardware/scripts/make_pcb.py`). The long technical reference stays in
`README.md` — section *Hardware Pinout Matrix*.

Useful when sketching a new layout: the **label** column is the name used in the code and on
the silkscreen; what you call each block on your drawing is up to you.

y 17.00→52.00): a 14-way body is 35.56 mm, so it stands 0.28 mm past each end of the
drawn slot, centred - pin 1 y 17.99 → pin 14 y 51.01 (33.02 = 13 × 2.54).

## 6. Small parts, what each one is for

Packages as placed: every `R`/`C` (except `C1`/`C3`) is **0805**, `C1`/`C3` are **SMD
aluminium cans 10 × 12.5 mm**, `D1` is **SOD-123**, `D2`/`D3` are **SMB**, `OK1`/`OK2`
are **SO-4 (4.4 × 3.9)**, `Q1` is **TO-263 / D²PAK**, `F1` is a **lateral 5 × 20 clip
holder** (THT). Everything except `F1` and `J3` is surface-mount; the `R`/`C`/`D`/`OK`/`Q1`
group sits on the top face, the plugs, the module and the two bulk cans `C1`/`C3`
underneath.

The 12 V chain reads top to bottom on the board: `J1` (XT30, underside, north edge) →
`F1` standing vertical over the module's middle → `Q1` just north of the clip, its pin
column above the module's `IN+` pad → `D1` / `R1` in the 4.4 mm strip west of that column
and `C2` / `D2` in the strip east of it, one column of the protection chain on each side
of the D²PAK (`C4` carries that east column on to the module's OUT end). `C13` (the 3V3
bypass) sits in the mid zone at the head of the 3V3 pin rather than in the west column.
Below all of it the mid band is a single lattice — see the *Small-parts lattice* row of
section 1 — so a signal's parts stack in a column instead of drifting.

## 7. Regenerating the board

```sh
python hardware/scripts/make_pcb.py        # writes the .kicad_pcb + footprints/dpp.pretty + drc-report.txt
python hardware/scripts/netsreport.py      # nets-report.md (per-net pad positions / wiring span)
python hardware/scripts/placementmap.py    # out/placement-map.png (courtyards + ratsnest MST)
python hardware/scripts/place.py <fp_id> <body_dia> [x y]   # legal free slots for a part
python hardware/scripts/dump.py               # placement table (courtyard + holes per part)
```

Rules are settled by **KiCad's own DRC** (`kicad-cli pcb drc`, run automatically at the end
of `make_pcb.py`; exit 0 and "Found 0 DRC violations" is the target). The bounding-box
notes the script prints are hints only - they are approximations, never the referee.

The distances this sheet quotes come from KiCad's own transforms: `nets-report.md` for pad
positions and sizes, the pad coordinates inside `drc-report.txt` for what a given rotation
really produces, and the `CrtYd` graphics in the board file for courtyards (SMD courtyards
here are a single `fp_rect`). `dpp:DPP_DCDC_MODULE` used to draw only a 0.4 mm north-edge
tick - a body-corner mark, not a courtyard: a 0.4 × 0.4 mm box leaves a 21 × 43 mm part
unpolicied, and it could not simply be widened, because the rectangle that box spans to the
opposite corner lies across the part, 43 mm away. It now carries a real courtyard: the drawn
body box plus the 0.25 mm margin, `x 12.00..33.50 / y 18.39..61.89`. Note it is *not* the
pad-outer-edge rectangle that a courtyard normally works out to be - these pads are drawn
inside the outline, so a pad-derived box would sit 1.50 mm inside the body along the long
edges and 0.75 mm inside along the short ones, and would police the wrong keep-out. Same-face
(B.Cu) clearances to it: 1.30 mm to `J1`, 0.50 mm to `C1`/`C3`, 3.11 mm to the south edge.
Opposite-face parts are not policed by the courtyard rule at all, which is what lets `F1`
straddle the module and `Q1` / `C2` / `D2` / `C4` overhang its east side; those were legal
before and stay legal. Probing confirms the rule is live rather than decorative: delete the
courtyard and nothing references it, add it and DRC still reports `Found 0 DRC violations`,
but widening the west edge by 1.25 mm lights up `courtyards_overlap` against `C1`/`C3`.
Do not derive these numbers with a hand-rolled rotation model: in KiCad's Y-down frame a
guessed sign on `rot 90` / `rot 270` silently swaps pad 1 and pad 2.

All coordinates come from `hardware/cad/Dashboard++ PCB with dimensions.geo.json`
(`hardware/scripts/extract_cad.py`), mirrored with `x = 95 − u`, `y = 10 + v`.
Those design-frame numbers are what the tables in `make_pcb.py`, this sheet and the
geo.json use; the emitters (`_txt`, `_dashrect`, the outline, the footprints, the
zone polygons) push them through `_onsheet()` by `PAGE_DX`/`PAGE_DY` at the moment
they are written, so the internal hints and the footprint library keep the small
familiar numbers while the sheet gets a centred drawing.
